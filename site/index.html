Current projects

What the lab is building

The Organizational Psychology Answers Lab (OPAL) at Central Connecticut State University builds tools that help students say what they can do, and studies whether those tools work.

Framework and tools

CR Path: the Career Readiness Pathway

A framework, published with James M. Conway, that describes career readiness as four components developed through iteration rather than in sequence:

  • Career-specific knowledge, skills, and abilities
  • Core competencies
  • Experiential learning opportunities
  • Career mentorship

Module 0 is the interactive entry point. Students work through reflective prompts, rename the framework in their own words, and leave with an articulation they can use in advising, interviews, and course planning. In fall 2026 it runs in courses across fourteen instructors and in Central Career Launch, the university's student employment program, where supervisors have been trained on it.

Funded pilot

SCALE: Scientific Communication + AI Literacy Experience

A pilot funded in 2026 by Connecticut's Tech Talent Accelerator. A spring 2027 course, Translational Literacy in Science, is co-delivered by natural science, social science, and library faculty, with discipline-specific modules from creators across the university. Students who earn them go on to employer-matched summer micro-internships.

SCALE is built on the Translational Literacy Framework: the capacity to read, evaluate, and communicate scientific evidence across disciplinary boundaries and from experts to the public, with generative AI treated as an instrument to be calibrated and questioned rather than trusted.

Immersive learning

XR for skill training and reflection

A faculty research grant supports a study of whether custom-calibrated XR meditation transfers to skill training, run with undergraduate research assistants in the university's XR Lab. Students in the college-to-career course also practice mock interviews in the lab with an AI interviewer asking the questions.

Faculty development and partnerships

Taking the framework beyond one department

Two campus workshops in July 2026 prepared faculty and staff from a dozen disciplines to deploy Module 0 in their own courses. The framework was the keynote at the Connecticut Department of Labor's Learns and Works symposium in April 2026, and the work continues through the Business-Higher Education Forum's Faculty Innovation Fellowship.

Teaching

Statistics with logged AI

Psychological Statistics and its lab were rebuilt for fall 2026 around one rule. Students must use AI to generate their own practice datasets and log every generation. They may not use it to interpret output or write results, because the interpretation is the assignment.

Whitepaper

Knowledge: the real durable skill

Employers say they want durable skills such as communication and critical thinking. This paper argues those are knowledge in use, and shows how the measurement tradition that could see knowledge was abandoned. It closes with practical moves for employers and educators.

About

Caleb B. Bragg

I am a professor of industrial/organizational psychology at Central Connecticut State University, where I have taught research methods since I was hired and now also teach psychological statistics, I/O psychology, and a college-to-career course built to close the gap between what students can do and what they can say.

My early research was on personality and counterproductive work behavior, including a trait self-control measure built for my dissertation. Since 2022 the work has centered on the college-to-career transition: the Career Readiness Pathway, the tools students use to walk it, and the Translational Literacy Framework that extends it into cross-disciplinary scientific communication. I use generative AI as a production layer for that work and teach students to calibrate it rather than trust it.

I served as the college's leadership fellow for career preparation and experiential learning, built its Board of Advisors, and replaced the university's typology-based career assessment with a validated instrument. I was a Faculty Innovation Fellow with the Business-Higher Education Forum and now lead the SCALE Pilot.
