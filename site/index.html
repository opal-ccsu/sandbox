Current projects

What the lab is building

The Organizational Psychology Answers Lab (OPAL) at Central Connecticut State University builds tools that help students say what they can do, and studies whether those tools work.

Framework and tools

CR Path: the Career Readiness Pathway

A framework, published with James M. Conway, that describes career readiness as four components developed through iteration rather than in sequence:

  • Career-specific knowledge, skills, and abilities
  • Core competencies
  • Experiential learning opportunities
  • Career mentorship

Module 0 is the interactive entry point. Students work through reflective prompts, rename the framework in their own words, and leave with an articulation they can use in advising, interviews, and course planning. In fall 2026 it runs in courses across fourteen instructors and in Central Career Launch, the university's student employment program, whose supervisors I trained to use it in August 2026.

Funded pilot

SCALE: Scientific Communication + AI Literacy Experience

A pilot funded by a Connecticut Tech Talent Accelerator 3.0 Innovation Grant, running September 2026 through August 2027. It teaches students to evaluate evidence across disciplines, test AI output against primary sources, and explain what they find to the people who make decisions.

  • Translational Literacy in Science, a 3-credit course, spring 2027
  • Co-taught by a natural scientist, a social scientist, and an AI librarian
  • Fifteen discipline modules written by CCSU faculty, released CC BY-NC 4.0
  • Paid summer micro-internships with Connecticut organizations

Lead partners are the New Britain Public Library and CT Humanities, with placements administered by CCSU's Central Workforce and Innovation Hub. The course is the first piece of a proposed Certificate in Applied Scientific Communication. SCALE is built on the Translational Literacy Framework.

Immersive learning

XR Meditation Skill Transfer study

A program of research in the CCSU XR Lab, begun in 2025, on whether a short guided meditation between two AI-powered mock interviews improves performance. In the first study, students' interview scores rose, state anxiety fell, and attitudes toward VR and AI grew more positive, with VR and in-person meditation producing similar results. Three undergraduate co-authors presented the first results with me at the Eastern Psychological Association's 2026 meeting.

The current study validates a VR meditation built at CCSU with a computer science student. In spring 2027, employers will try the protocol and say whether it belongs in hiring, training, and onboarding. Funded by a 2026–2027 CSU-AAUP Faculty Research Grant, in partnership with the XR Lab and the Central Workforce and Innovation Hub.

Taking the framework beyond one department

In July 2026 I ran two campus workshops that prepared faculty and staff from a dozen disciplines to use Module 0 in their own courses. In April 2026 I gave the keynote on the framework at the Connecticut Department of Labor's Learns and Works symposium.

Whitepaper

Knowledge: the real durable skill

Employers say they want durable skills such as communication and critical thinking. I argue those are knowledge in use, and show how the measurement tradition that could see knowledge was abandoned. It closes with practical moves for employers and educators.

About

Caleb B. Bragg

I am a professor of industrial/organizational psychology at Central Connecticut State University. I was hired to teach I/O psychology and research methods, and I now also teach psychological statistics and a college-to-career course built to close the gap between what students can do and what they can say.

My early research was on personality and counterproductive work behavior, including a trait self-control measure built for my dissertation. Since 2022 the work has centered on the college-to-career transition: the Career Readiness Pathway, the tools students use to walk it, and the Translational Literacy Framework that extends it into cross-disciplinary scientific communication. I use generative AI as a production layer for that work and teach students to calibrate it rather than trust it.

I served as the college's leadership fellow for career preparation and experiential learning, was instrumental in building its Board of Advisors, and replaced the university's typology-based career assessment with a validated instrument. I was a Faculty Innovation Fellow with the Business-Higher Education Forum and now lead the SCALE Pilot.
