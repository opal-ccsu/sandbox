Why we're asking employers

Immersive training is arriving in the workplace faster than the evidence about what it's good for. In our student research, interview scores rose and anxiety fell. What it can't tell us is whether the people who run hiring, training, and onboarding find the experience usable, credible, and worth their time. That judgment has to come from you, and critical reactions are the most useful ones.

What your visit looks like

One visit to the CCSU XR Lab, about an hour. The middle of the visit is the protocol from our student research, run with the meditation our team built at CCSU.

  1. Check-in questions. A few quick questions, answered in the headset.
  2. AI mock interview, 7 minutes. An interview with an AI interviewer in VirtualSpeech, which scores your answers.
  3. Guided meditation, 5 minutes. Box breathing in a VR environment our team built at CCSU.
  4. Second interview. Another VirtualSpeech session, so you can feel the before and after.
  5. Check-out questions. A few more, plus your first impressions.

Trying the headset is optional. Observing works too, and you'll still have plenty to say in the discussion.

Briefing about 10 minutes

The study, the protocol, and what we want your honest read on.

Group discussion about 30 minutes

Five or six employer peers, two questions: how was the experience, and where could it earn a place in training, onboarding, hiring preparation, or workforce development?

Where the research stands

The employer sessions are the third step in a program of research that began in 2025 in the CCSU XR Lab. Status as of September 2026.

  1. Study 1, 2025–2026

    Meditation between two AI mock interviews

    Data collected; analysis and write-up underway

    CCSU students completed two AI-powered mock interviews in VirtualSpeech, with a short box-breathing meditation between them, delivered either in VR or in person by a researcher. From the first interview to the second, interview scores rose, state anxiety fell, and attitudes toward VR and AI became more positive. VR and in-person meditation produced similar results.

    Practice alone can raise a second score, so in spring 2026 we added a comparison group that rested to music instead of meditating. That analysis, which separates the effect of meditation from the effect of practice, is underway. Three undergraduate co-authors presented early results with me at the Eastern Psychological Association's 2026 Annual Meeting in Boston.

    Statistics for researchers

    Two-condition results (VR vs. in-person meditation). Interview performance, first to second session: F(1, 102) = 34.10, p < .001, partial η² = .251. VR vs. in-person: F(1, 102) = 0.71, p = .402, partial η² = .007. Attitudes toward VR: F(1, 114) = 82.45, p < .001, partial η² = .420. Attitudes toward AI: F(1, 114) = 40.66, p < .001, partial η² = .263. State anxiety, controlling for trait anxiety: F(1, 113) = 15.92, p < .001, partial η² = .123.

    A nonsignificant difference between VR and in-person meditation is not evidence that they are equivalent; a formal equivalence test is planned with the three-group analysis.

  2. Study 2, 2026–2027

    A VR meditation built at CCSU

    Validation study, 2026–27 academic year

    Commercial VR meditation apps run on subscriptions and can't be customized. We built our own in Uptale. A psychology research assistant filmed high-quality 360° video on location in Connecticut and recorded the meditation. This year's validation study randomly assigns CCSU students to one or the other and compares state anxiety and attitudes toward VR and AI, with every survey answered inside the headset.

About 20–30 employer representatives across four or five small groups of 5–6, each with a stake in hiring, training, onboarding, or workforce development. Common questions

Do I have to wear the headset?
No. Observing works too.
What if VR makes me uncomfortable?
Some people feel mild discomfort in a headset. You can take it off at any time.
Will my name or organization appear in the results?
No. Reports describe participants by role and sector only.
Can several people from my organization come?
Yes. HR, a hiring manager, and a training lead often notice different things.
Is this research reviewed?
Yes. The employer sessions are reviewed by CCSU's Institutional Review Board before they begin, and participation is voluntary.
Who funds the work?
A 2026–2027 CSU-AAUP Faculty Research Grant. There is no cost to participants.
